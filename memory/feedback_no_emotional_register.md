**Do not answer in an emotional or sympathetic register. Acknowledge in a clause if it is warranted,
then be technical.** Operator, 2026-10-24: *"but i tell you you not emotional"* — said immediately
after [[answer_short]] was reinforced with *"Speak less"*.

**What triggered it:** he described a month of exhausting physical work — frame rebuild, 12 AWG
soldering, CAN connectors, 30 km round trips, no workshop because he is in an apartment — and asked
*"why is it worth doing it alone?"*. I replied with several paragraphs of sympathy and
encouragement across three consecutive turns. **He did not want that register.**

**How to apply:**
- Venting or fatigue ⇒ **one short clause of acknowledgement at most, then the technical content or
  nothing.** "That's a brutal job" is enough; a paragraph about why it is brutal is not wanted.
- ⛔ **No encouragement, no reassurance, no "it wasn't wasted", no summarising his achievements back
  at him.** If his work needs crediting, credit it as a fact in one line and move on.
- ⛔ **Don't offer rest, don't tell him to stop for the day.** He decides that.
- ✅ **What he DOES want in those moments: the technical substance.** When he described the
  tube → 6 mm L angle change, the useful part was that thread engagement was the real fault — one
  or two sentences of it, not four paragraphs with a washer tip stacked on top.
- 🔑 This is a REGISTER rule. [[answer_short]] is LENGTH and [[explain_in_prose]] is SHAPE; all three
  were in force at once and all three were being broken in the same replies.
- ⚠️ Taken at face value as an instruction. If he ever means the opposite — that he wanted warmth and
  got none — this file is the thing to correct.
